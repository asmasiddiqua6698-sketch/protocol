Period cravings: what to eat instead

Cravings are strongest around your period, so plan for them rather than fight them. Keep portions small and have the snack after a meal, not instead of one. Skip chocolate, coffee and mint, as they can trigger reflux.

  • Sweet craving: 1 whole fruit (guava, papaya, apple or pear), or 1 katori curd with a pinch of cinnamon and chopped fruit. Or 1 date with 5 almonds or 2 walnut halves.
  • Warm and comforting: a cup of warm vegetable and dal soup, or a small bowl of moong dal khichdi with curd. Chamomile tea in the evening, or a thin slice of ginger in warm water after meals.
  • Salty or crunchy: 1 small handful of roasted chana or roasted makhana with a little salt and pepper, or a glass of buttermilk with roasted jeera and a pinch of salt.
  • Iron and magnesium: spinach or methi sabzi, whole moong or chana, eggs, and 1 tbsp ground pumpkin seeds in curd. Keep tea and coffee away from iron-rich meals.
  • Evening hunger: a boiled egg with cucumber slices, or curd with your cycle-phase seeds.
  • Never skip meals during your period, as this makes cravings stronger. Fish twice a week and a heat pack on your lower abdomen can help with cramps.
  • If cramps stop you doing daily activities, or bleeding is heavy or has clots, tell Dietitian Asma and your doctor.

Breakfast: eggs and vegetables every day

Eggs are your daily anchor. Eat the vegetables first, then the eggs, and a small roti last only if you are still hungry. Skip poha and upma for now.

Basic veggie omelette: whisk 2 eggs with salt, pepper and a pinch of turmeric. Saute half a cup of finely chopped vegetables in half a tsp oil for 2 to 3 minutes. Pour in the eggs, cover and cook on low for 3 minutes, then fold.

  • Spinach omelette: add chopped spinach to the pan for the last minute of sauteing.
  • Mushroom omelette: saute sliced mushrooms until the water dries before adding the eggs.
  • Mushroom and spinach omelette, with beans if you like.
  • Methi leaves and carrot omelette.
  • Grated zucchini or lauki with carrot omelette.
  • Beans and carrot omelette.
  • Egg bhurji: scramble 2 eggs on low heat with sauteed spinach and grated zucchini.
  • Gym mornings: 1 whole egg plus 2 whites with mushrooms and spinach, for extra protein.
  • Short on time: 2 boiled eggs with sauteed beans and moong sprouts.
  • Capsicum is optional. Swap it for beans or zucchini if it causes burning.
  • Serve with a small roti (jowar, ragi or multigrain) only if hungry.

1. Rice and quinoa mix (resistant starch)

Cook this twice in the week, for example on Sunday and Wednesday.

  • Take 1 cup brown rice and half a cup quinoa. Rinse both well. Soak the rice for 30 minutes.
  • Cook together in a pressure cooker or pot with about 3 cups water and a pinch of salt until soft and fluffy.
  • Spread on a plate to cool for about 30 minutes, then refrigerate overnight. The cooling creates the resistant starch.
  • Scoop out one portion and reheat until warm with a splash of water. Reheat only what you will eat.
  • Keep covered in the fridge and finish within 2 days. Start with the smaller portion and increase slowly if your stomach is comfortable.

2. Chicken: marinate once, cook two ways

Cut 500 g boneless chicken breast or thigh into cubes. Choose one marinade.

Classic curd marinade

3 tbsp thick curd, half tsp turmeric, 1 tsp jeera powder, 1 tsp coriander powder, 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste, half tsp black pepper, salt, 1 tsp oil.

Green coriander marinade

Blend a handful of coriander leaves, a few curry leaves, a small piece of ginger and 2 tbsp curd into a paste. Mix with the chicken, a pinch of turmeric, salt and pepper.

Ajwain marinade

Mix curd, salt, half tsp ajwain, pepper, half tsp turmeric and 1 tsp kasuri methi.

Marinate for at least 30 minutes, or overnight in the fridge.

Air fry

180 C for 12 to 15 minutes, turning halfway, until cooked through with no pink inside.

Pan fry

  • Heat a non-stick pan on medium with half a tsp oil. Add the chicken in a single layer.
  • Cover and cook 6 to 7 minutes, turn, and cook uncovered another 5 to 6 minutes until golden. Cut one large piece to check there is no pink.

Cooked chicken keeps 2 days in the fridge. Reheat in the air fryer for 3 to 4 minutes or in a covered pan.

3. Fish: air fried or pan fried

Good choices are salmon, mackerel (bangda), sardines (tarli), rohu or basa fillets. Buy it fresh, cook it the same or next day, and do not keep cooked fish more than 1 day.

Easy marinade (2 fillets or 250 g)

Half tsp turmeric, half tsp ajwain or jeera powder, half tsp ginger-garlic paste, a pinch of black pepper, salt, and 1 tsp curd or oil to bind. No lime or vinegar. Marinate 20 to 30 minutes only.

Air fry

180 C for 8 to 10 minutes for fillets, or 10 to 12 minutes for steaks, turning once. The fish should flake easily.

Pan fry

Heat a non-stick pan on medium with half a tsp oil. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side, covered for the first side, and do not move it too much so it does not break. Serve with sauteed vegetables and a small portion of the rice and quinoa mix.

4. Other quick proteins

  • Egg and vegetable stir-fry: saute jeera, chopped beans and carrot, add 2 beaten eggs, stir until set, and season with salt and pepper.
  • Tofu or paneer pan fry: cube, toss with turmeric, jeera, salt and pepper, and pan fry in half a tsp oil for 4 to 5 minutes until lightly golden. Keep paneer to a small portion.
  • Soy chunks sabzi: soak 30 g soy chunks in hot water for 10 minutes, squeeze dry, and saute with jeera, turmeric, coriander powder, salt and chopped vegetables.

5. Easy complex carbs

  • Quinoa vegetable pulao: saute jeera and chopped beans, carrot and peas in 1 tsp oil. Add 1 cup rinsed quinoa and 2 cups water, cover and cook 15 minutes. Serve with curd.
  • Oats vegetable chilla: blend 1 cup oats into powder. Mix with 1 egg or 2 tbsp curd, grated carrot, chopped spinach, salt and jeera, and enough water for a pouring batter. Cook on a non-stick tawa with a few drops of oil.
  • Moong dal and brown rice khichdi: pressure cook half a cup moong dal, half a cup brown rice, chopped vegetables, turmeric, jeera and salt with about 4 cups water. Eat with curd. Good as a light dinner.
  • Ragi or jowar roti if you can get them fresh, keeping to 1 medium roti.
  • Whole chana or rajma: boil and lightly saute with jeera and turmeric as a side. Increase slowly if you get bloating.

6. Easy vegetables and salads

Cooked is gentler on your stomach right now.

  • Jeera beans and carrot: saute jeera in 1 tsp oil, add chopped beans and carrot with salt and turmeric, cover and cook 8 to 10 minutes.
  • Lauki sabzi: cook cubed lauki with jeera, turmeric and salt until soft, about 10 minutes.
  • Palak and mushroom stir-fry: saute sliced mushrooms until the water dries, add chopped spinach and cook 2 minutes with salt and pepper.
  • Air or pan fried bhindi, beans or zucchini: toss with half a tsp oil, salt, pepper and a little jeera. Air fry at 180 C for 10 to 12 minutes, or pan fry on medium for 8 to 10 minutes.
  • Warm sprouts salad: lightly saute moong sprouts with jeera and salt for 3 to 4 minutes. Mix with steamed beans, grated carrot and a few cucumber slices. Add your cycle-phase ground seeds and a tiny drizzle of olive oil. No lime or vinegar.
  • Steamed beetroot and carrot salad: steam until soft, cool, and toss with salt, pepper, roasted jeera and your seeds.
  • Vegetable and dal soup: boil mixed vegetables with a spoon of moong dal, turmeric and jeera, and blend lightly if you prefer. Good for dinner.

Movement, stretching and calm

Gentle, regular movement supports fat loss, blood sugar, digestion, sleep and stress together. Stop any movement that causes pain, dizziness or burning, and tell Dietitian Asma.

Daily movement

  • Steps: build toward 10,000 steps a day as an average. Walk during calls and get up for a few minutes every hour.
  • Strength training: 2 to 3 times a week as per your gym plan. Have your pre-gym snack 45 to 60 minutes before, and never train after a skipped meal.
  • After dinner: a 20-minute easy walk. Do not use the workout as a reason to eat more.
  • After meals: keep movement gentle. Avoid deep forward bends or lying flat right after eating, as this can bring on reflux.
  • During your period: walking and easy stretching are enough. Lower the intensity if you feel tired or crampy, and use a heat pack for cramps.

10-minute stretching routine

Morning or evening, at least 2 hours after a meal. Hold each stretch for 20 to 30 seconds, breathe slowly, and never push into pain.

  • Neck side stretch, each side
  • Shoulder rolls, then clasp hands behind your back and open the chest
  • Cat-cow, 8 slow rounds
  • Child's pose with knees wide, which also eases period cramps
  • Low lunge stretch for the front of the hip, each side
  • Seated forward stretch for the back of the legs, each leg
  • Lying spinal twist with knees to one side, each side
  • Calf stretch against a wall, each leg

Meditation and breathing

  • 4-6 breathing: breathe in for 4 seconds and out for 6. Do this for 2 to 5 minutes between work blocks, before meals or when stressed.
  • 5-minute seated meditation: sit comfortably, close your eyes and follow your breath. When your mind wanders, gently return to the breath. Do it after waking or before bed, and build to 10 minutes over a few weeks.
  • Body scan at bedtime: lie down, and slowly relax each body part from toes to head for 5 minutes.

Sleep

  • Keep a consistent sleep and wake time, aiming for 7 to 9 hours in bed.
  • Get outdoor light soon after waking.
  • Avoid skipped meals, dehydration and late caffeine, as they can bring on migraines.
